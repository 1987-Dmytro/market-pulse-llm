### 5b.2 — the batch measurement failed by the rule ($0.4277 of pod, $1.7069 cumulative)

The carve ladder ran five arms on 24 rows: the repeat control identical to itself, and **16, 8 and
4 all byte-identical to batch 1** — so the rule selected **16**. The paid run scored `comments_test`
**400/400** and `posts_test` **250/250** at batch 16 with zero failures, then died on
`torch.OutOfMemoryError`: 1.85 GiB requested, 432 MiB free, **42.14 GiB genuinely allocated** against
a ~20 GiB model at rest — a real ~22 GiB working set, not mainly fragmentation.

**The adoption rule then had no input.** It requires every 4.5h2-passed gate to stay passing; G1b is
the sarcasm-holdout slice and 16 of its 108 rows were scored. An unmeasured gate is not a passing
one, so `results/batch_5b2_verdict.json` reads `outcome: failed-measurement-oom` and **serving is
batch 1 permanently** — only a new pre-registration may move it. Salvaged from the checkpoint's 666
rows: **660/666 label-identical** to batch 1, G1a −0.0022, G1c +0.0031, G1d/G1e +0.0000, both
computable deltas inside the 0.005 tolerance, G1b reported as **missing rather than estimated**.
**1028 passed**, 12 deviations. ADR: [[5b2-batch-measurement]].

**Two readings were refused out loud in the record.** That batch 16 "only needs more memory" — six
rows moved that the 24-row carve swore could not, which is the phase's real finding. And that G1c
at 0.8509 clearing its 0.8483 bar means anything — four flipped rows on a head already inside a
rounding error, and adopting a configuration because it flatters a gate is exactly what the
pre-registration exists to prevent. This bounds [[phase4-own-pod-anchor]] §(c) rather than
repealing it: different model state, different stack.

A late fix (`9be36e4`) closed a verify-gate item the report was about to claim as passed: the
verdict was purpose-built by hand — correctly, so it could not read as the measurement — and
therefore skipped the shared stamper, shipping with the adapter sha, the prompt hashes and **no
commit**. It now carries two, because they are two facts: this machine when the salvage ran, and
the pod's checkout `0a0e09c7` that generated the rows.
